# Gotchas — Billing, credits, payments and plans

Moved verbatim from `CLAUDE.md` (the rule headlines stay there). Read this file before changing `Modules/Billing/`, `Modules/Payments/`, credits, withholding, webhooks, subscriptions. Bullets that say «above» may now point into another file in `docs/gotchas/`.

- **A credit is one session at ONE teacher's rate, and the account it hangs off is platform-wide.** `student_credit_accounts`, `credit_packages` and `terms_consents` carry **no** `workspace_id` by design — one person, one account, one catalogue, one signature. The BALANCE underneath is workspace-scoped, because a credit is not portable between teachers. Adding `BelongsToWorkspace` to the account would silently duplicate one person per teacher, which is the mirror-image bug `PlatformOwnershipTest` exists to catch in both directions.
- **The floor guards BOOKING, never the recording of a debt.** `CreditMovement::$enforceFloor` defaults to **false**: a session that was delivered is owed whether or not the student can pay for it — spec 014 has already earned the teacher their fee from the same `SessionDelivered` event, so refusing the entry would leave the platform owing money with no claim against anyone. A test that expects `InsufficientCreditsException` must pass `enforceFloor: true` or it is asserting the wrong requirement.
- **`is_withheld` is derived from FIVE inputs and stored as none of them**: the balance, the ceiling, the billing mode, an open exam window, and a CURRENT terms consent. So a lift is instantaneous and no `access_holds` table can drift from it — and a published new version of the terms stops deferral for everyone who has not signed it, on their next booking, with nothing to sweep. Never add a column for it; `WithholdingReader::stamp()` is the bulk form, and a per-row read inside a Resource is an N+1 by construction.
- **The credit-limit demotion IS the switch to prepaid for that student.** The floor is `−credit_limit_credits`, so a ceiling of zero and a prepaid mode produce the identical predicate; a per-student `mode` column would be a second answer to a question the floor already answers. And the limit moves by **deltas** with the earning counter CONSUMED — a `limit = f(consent, history)` recomputed from scratch hands the defaulted student their ceiling back on their first payment afterwards. The initial `0 → 1` is an EVENT (first consent, and the birth of any later balance), never a state anything derives.
- **`isBlocked` asks the balance as well as the floor.** `$floor < 0` was the whole test for "owes money" and the demotion breaks it: the ceiling goes to zero while the balance stays negative, so in a `remind` workspace the defaulted student books at −3, −4, for ever — exactly the person the demotion was aimed at.
- **Withholding is per COURSE, never per person, and balances are never summed.** +10 in maths and −6 in physics reads as +4 and unblocked when added up, while the design withholds per course precisely so the paid-up course stays open. The API sends no total because there is no correct one.
- **No money reaches the student's or the teacher's screen.** A credit's price is the teacher's approved settlement rate plus two platform constants, so a total shown to either side is solvable for the other's rate across two package sizes. `StudentBalanceAllowlist` and `PublicFieldAllowlist` fail the build over a field that leaks it, and `formatMinorMoney()` on the frontend is the only place a number becomes currency.
- **Approving a credit purchase and moving a ceiling are PLATFORM permissions**, held by no tenant role. A package a teacher could define is a sale price a teacher sets (FR-021ب); a ceiling a teacher could raise is a teacher deciding how much the platform may be owed. `billing.credits.adjust` currently has no HTTP surface at all — the Action exists and enforces its own rules, and a screen for it has not been built.
- **Reconciliation is a JOB, and the obvious check inside it is blind.** Three `GROUP BY`s over the fastest-growing tables with no tenant filter would run on every refresh of a page. And the ledger-vs-balance comparison proves almost nothing on its own: both sides are written by the same path in the same transaction, so a session that was never charged leaves them in perfect agreement. The two invariants that see it come from outside — one consumption entry per seat of a charged session, and a positive balance equalling what its lots hold.
- **An expiry claims its lot first and then posts with `drawsFromLots: false`.** Posting it as an ordinary negative movement lets the drawer take the same credits a second time out of lots that have not expired. Expiry is switched OFF at launch (`validity_days` defaults to null) but the soonest-expiring-first draw order is live from day one — turning expiry on later must not rewrite which lot paid for which past session. ⛔ **And it never expires a credit that is HOLDING a seat (2026-09-27):** only `remaining − held` on the balance may lapse, so a lot can expire in parts — the held part stays in the (expired, therefore first-drawn) lot until its seat is charged or released. Each part is its own idempotency key (`credit_lot`, then `credit_lot_2`, `credit_lot_3`…): a second part under the first part's key would be swallowed as «already recorded» and leave the lot emptied with no entry behind it.
- **The dormancy notice needs its own column.** The notice moves no credits, so a predicate on `last_transaction_at` alone re-sends it every night to the student least likely to want it; `notified_dormant_at` is stamped BEFORE the dispatch (a lost reminder beats one a night for ever) and cleared by the ledger on the next movement. It is a reminder, never an expiry: nothing is forfeited and nothing moves to another teacher, which would be a re-pricing.
- **The entry is written BEFORE the lots are drawn and before the balance moves.** Decrementing first means a redelivered event debits twice while the duplicate entry is ignored once — and the balance stops equalling the sum of its entries, permanently, with nothing to notice it. `ReconcileCreditBalancesJob` is what would notice, the next night, by which time the cause is a week of traffic away.
- **A valid signature is not a check on the amount, and the two get conflated the moment one class does both.** A provider can sign a perfectly correct notification saying 100 for a payment of 500 — the signature proves the body came from them, not that the body agrees with the order. `HandleProviderCallback` compares the amount separately and answers `mismatch`: no charge, no credits, the withholding stays, and the case enters the reconciliation report. A verifier that returned "valid" for both questions would credit the student 100 and close the debt of 500.
- **A provider with no gateway accepts no notification at all.** `manual` has none, so anything arriving at `/webhooks/payments/manual` naming a real reference is an impersonation, not a late callback — it is refused with zero effect, and an unknown provider is a `404`. Both answers are `202`-shaped from outside for the reason the signature is: a distinct reply for a wrong signature is an oracle telling an attacker when they are getting warm.
- **One capture per order is a UNIQUE COLUMN, not a partial index.** `captured_order_id` is nullable and carries `unique()`; it is written `= order_id` inside the same atomic conditional UPDATE that moves the status to captured, and NULL does not collide with NULL, so every non-captured row coexists freely. MySQL has no partial indexes — `WHERE status = 'captured'` on an index is a Postgres feature — so the "obvious" form of this guard silently does not exist on the database this ships to. The column is deliberately NOT `$fillable`: mass-assignable, it becomes a second way to claim the lock from outside the transaction that owns it.
- **The callback's idempotency key is TWO columns, `(provider, external_id)`.** An id is unique within the provider that issued it and nowhere else, so a single-column key means the second gateway ever integrated starts silently swallowing the first one's notifications as duplicates. The sweep exploits the same index deliberately: it REPLAYS the notification the provider should have sent rather than capturing by hand, so a sweep and a late callback for one payment collapse into one row.
- **`(int) null` IS `0`, AND ON A SUBSCRIPTION'S DURATION THAT IS A PLAN THAT EXPIRES THE INSTANT IT IS ACTIVATED — SO THE GUARD BELONGS AT THE BRANCH, NEVER AT THE DTO.** Spec 036 made a plan «either a window of days OR a number of hours, never both and never neither», which turned `plans.duration_days` nullable — and every reader that wrote `(int) $plan->duration_days` kept compiling, kept passing, and started computing `now()->addDays(0)`: a month's access sold, paid for, and over before the student's first lesson, with nothing failing anywhere. **The fix is not a cast and not a validation rule.** A `required` on the form request is a guard the panel, the seeders and any future importer walk straight past — the repository's own «the Action is the single entrance» rule — and a DTO that refuses a null duration refuses the hours shape outright, which is the feature. What is correct is the BRANCH: the shape decides which column is read, the hours shape never touches the duration at all, and `SavePlan::resolveShape()` refuses a row carrying both or neither, once, where every door already arrives. ⚠️ **And a nullable column that used to be NOT NULL invalidates every docblock written about it** — `PlanShape` printed «null يوماً» at a buyer for the same reason, and `null % 30 === 0` is TRUE in JavaScript, so the frontend's month branch swallowed it and printed the literal word `null` on a price card.
- **A BOOLEAN THAT ANSWERS A QUESTION WHICH HAS QUIETLY GROWN A THIRD ANSWER SWALLOWS THAT ANSWER AT EVERY READER, AND IT WAS SEVEN OF THEM.** `PlanCoverage::needsCourse()` was true and correct while coverage was «the whole workspace» or «one course». Spec 036 added «one group» — and the method still answered a two-valued question, so a cohort plan fell into the «no uuid needed» branch: `SavePlan` **NULLED** `coverage_uuid` on the way to the database and saved a group plan pointing at no group. Saved, listed on the teacher's own screen, reaching nothing, while the group it was written for read «no plan reaches me» — two screens contradicting each other with nothing logged. ⚠️ **The rename IS the fix, not tidying**: `requiresUuid()` asks what every caller was really asking, so the third case answers it correctly without a single call site being re-reasoned. Read literally, the old name is still true — that is what makes it dangerous. ⚠️ **And the `match` carries no `default` and matches on the CASE rather than on `->value`**, which is what makes the analyser name every unhandled arm the day a fourth coverage lands; a `default`, or a comparison against the string, is silent and the new case quietly behaves like the first one. The check when adding a case to any enum: grep its own methods for a `bool` return before you grep the callers.
- **A LOT IS OPENED BY THE SIGN OF A MOVEMENT, NEVER BY ITS TYPE — and a type list left two credits with no batch behind them.** `CreditLedger::post()` opened a lot only for `Purchase` and `Bonus`, while the excuse reversal (`EloquentSessionSeatCharges::reverse()`, an `Adjustment` of +1) and a positive `AdjustCredits` raised `remaining_credits` too. The lots fell short of the balance, so `ReconcileCreditBalancesJob` reported a false `lot_remainder` for that student every night, and with expiry switched on that credit could never expire, because expiry walks lots. Every `credits > 0` movement opens one now. Its expiry is the caller's decision, made the way packages make it: a purchase carries its package's validity, a bonus or a manual correction carries none, and a REVERSED CHARGE carries the expiry of the lot(s) the charge drew from (`credit_allocations` → `credit_lots`) — the latest of them, or none if any was undated or the charge drew from no lot at all.
- **`subscriptions.ends_on` IS INCLUSIVE — the last day that opens — so N days end on `starts + N − 1`.** `ActivateSubscription::claim()` wrote `addDays(N)`, and with `liveOn()` admitting `effective_ends_on >= today` a 30-day plan ran 31 days (15 Oct → 14 Nov), one more on every renewal. Freeze periods were already dated `starts_on + days − 1`; the two date columns now mean the same thing. And a renewal already bought is RE-DATED when a later freeze extends the month before it (`EffectiveSubscriptionEnd::redateChain()`, per plan lineage via `PlanLineage`, only while the renewal has not started): the window moves whole — `starts_on` and `ends_on` by the same days — so «`ends_on` never moves» is now «the LENGTH never moves». Before it, the frozen week pushed the first month into the renewal and access still stopped on the renewal's original date.
- **A GATEWAY-CAPTURED ORDER NEVER READS `approved`, so «has this order been taken back?» is `cancelled`/`rejected`, never «not approved».** `HandleProviderCallback` captures the payment and fires `PaymentCaptured` without passing through `ApproveOrder`, so the order stays `pending`. The queued listeners that open access (`CreateEnrollmentFromOrder`, `ActivateSubscription`) re-read the row through `Order::wasWithdrawn()` — a reversal landing between the approval and the worker otherwise left an active enrolment on a cancelled order — and a check written as `! isApproved()` there would have refused every gateway buyer their purchase. A credit sale (package or hours plan) is reversed by `ReverseCreditOrder`, which claims THIS purchase's lot and posts with `drawsFromLots: false` (the expiry rule above): only what the lot still holds goes back, so consumed credits stay consumed.
- **CANCELLING A PLAN REFUNDS ITS UNUSED PART ONLY, AND THE AMOUNT IS A COLUMN — `payment_transactions.refunded_minor` (owner decision 2026-09-27, reversing «a cancellation refunds the whole payment»).** A duration subscription (`CancelSubscription`) gives back `paid × unused ÷ length`: length = `ends_on − starts_on + 1` (the days paid for; a freeze moves the end, never the length), unused = from the platform-zone cancellation date — which counts as UNUSED, access stops at that moment — through `effective_ends_on` inclusive, capped at the length; cancelled before its first day ⇒ everything back. An hours plan (`ReverseCreditOrder`, session-shaped orders only) gives back `paid × unused sessions ÷ sessions bought`, the unused sessions being exactly the credits its lot still holds. Both FLOOR to the minor unit by integer division, and `paid` is the CAPTURED amount (after coupons), never the list price. The one spelling is `Payments\Support\SubscriptionRefund`; the Filament confirmations read it through `CancelSubscription::refundPreview()` / `ReverseCreditOrder::moneyRefundFor()`, never an estimate beside it. ⚠️ **The capture still flips to `reversed` as one act**, so «reversed» no longer means «all of it went back»: `ReversePayment(…, $refundMinor)` writes `refunded_minor` (null ⇒ the whole amount — a chargeback, a credit package, a course order), the migration backfilled every older `reversed` row to its full amount, and the collection report sums it per group. ⚠️ **The teacher keeps every delivered session's pay** — settlement never sees a subscription and nothing on this path reverses a unit; the part the platform keeps is what that pay came out of. A credit PACKAGE reversal is unchanged: the whole capture.
- **A SUBSCRIPTION THAT ENDS EARLY RELEASES THE SEATS IT WAS PAYING FOR, WHATEVER ELSE THE STUDENT HOLDS IN THE COURSE (2026-09-27).** A subscriber's seats carry NO credit hold (027 · FR-041). `SubscriptionAccess::close()` announced `SubscriptionEnded` only when it closed an enrolment — and a student who had ALSO bought the course outright keeps that enrolment, so nothing was announced and six future seats stayed booked with nothing behind them, each charged −1 at delivery with the floor off. The event now always carries the subscription's window (covered courses, session type, first instant after its last day) and `ReleaseSeatsOnSubscriptionEnd::releaseSeatsItPaidFor()` releases the seats in it with no open hold (`SessionCreditHolds::openHoldSessionIds()`) that no live subscription covers at their start. A reschedule past the subscriber's end does the same for the moved seat (`UpdateClassSession::releaseSeatsMovedPastTheirSubscription()`). ⚠️ «No hold» alone is not «a subscription seat» — a cash-collecting workspace books with no hold too — which is why the window bounds it.
- **`Subscription::scopeLiveOn($moment)` judges the STATUS at the moment as well as the dates (2026-09-27).** `expired` is live through `effective_ends_on` (the sweep writes it only after), `cancelled` is live only while `cancelled_at > $moment`. Asked about `now()` nothing changes; asked about a lesson's start it stops charging a credit for a lesson a since-expired or later-cancelled subscription paid for — and the charge (`coveringSessionFor`) and the pay (`subscriberIdsAmong`) agree because both ask it.
- **MONEY IS TYPED AND SHOWN IN MAJOR UNITS THROUGH THE MODEL'S VIRTUAL ATTRIBUTE; THE `*_minor` COLUMN STAYS THE STORED TRUTH, AND A FORM NEVER CONVERTS (owner decision 2026-09-27).** #266 put the ×100 in the Filament FORM (`MoneyInput` hydrated and dehydrated), which worked and left the rule in the wrong place: every new screen had to remember to go through that one field, and the coupon form read the STORED `value_kind` to fill while it read the LIVE one to save — so a fixed coupon of 2500 switched to a percent opened on «2500٪». Now each money model declares a virtual attribute over its column — `Plan::price`, `Course::price`, `Subscription::price` over `price_minor`, `Order::amount` and `PaymentTransaction::amount` over `amount_minor`, `PaymentTransaction::refunded_amount` (read-only, because `refunded_minor` has one writer) — built once by `MinorUnits::attribute('price_minor')`: it reads «49.99» (a STRING, never a float) and writes 4999; blank is null; anything filled that is not a number with at most two decimals THROWS rather than storing a null that would take a priced plan off sale. `Coupon::amount` is hand-written because `value` has two meanings: it reads null for a percent coupon, and the form shows the percent box OR the amount box by the live kind, the hidden one never saved. Four things are load-bearing. **The attributes are NOT in `$appends`**, so no API payload and no `toArray()` changed; the API, the Actions, reports and settlement read `*_minor` exactly as before. **`Plan::price` and `Course::price` are NOT `$fillable`** — `price_minor` is deliberately not fillable on a plan (a teacher's `SavePlan` array is one key away from the platform's number), and a fillable second spelling of that column reopens the door; `EditCourse` assigns `$record->price` explicitly and `CreatePlan`/`EditPlan` hand the Action `MinorUnits::fromMajorOrFail($data['price'])`. `Coupon::amount` IS fillable, because both coupon pages are Filament's defaults and a non-fillable key is discarded in silence. **Filament's edit page fills from `attributesToArray()`, which carries no virtual attribute**, so `MoneyInput` asks the record for its own attribute by the field's name when it hydrates — a read, not a conversion; without it the box opens empty on a priced plan and the save writes null. **`numeric()` hands the setter a FLOAT** (`NumberStateCast`), and `number_format()` rounds — so `MinorUnits::fromMajor()` refuses a float that does not survive the trip to two decimals (49.999 is refused, never stored as 5000). The platform fees are `platform_settings` rows, not a column, so `BillingSettings` — the class that owns those keys — converts them (`operatingFee()` / `setOperatingFee()`, `gatewayFixedFee()` / `setGatewayFixedFee()`), and the admin API keeps writing minor units. «Two decimals» is written once, in `MinorUnits::DECIMALS`.
- **NO RECEIPT, NO APPROVAL: `ApproveOrder` CLAIMS ON `Order::scopeApprovable()` — `under_review`, or a `pending` order whose amount is ZERO — AND NEVER ON `awaitingDecision()` (owner decision 2026-09-28).** `pending` means the student has not paid yet; `under_review` is written by `UploadPaymentReceipt` and by nothing else. The claim used to be `awaitingDecision()` (both statuses), so the subscription queue on `GrantCreditSubscription` showed «لا إيصال» with «اعتمد» right beside it, and one press opened a course or minted credits for money nobody had sent. The rule lives in three places that each do a different job: the **claim's WHERE clause** is the guard (a stale read cannot slip past a conditional UPDATE); a **fresh-row read before the cohort checks** (`refuseWithoutReceipt()`) exists only so the officer reads «لا يمكن اعتماد طلب لم يُرفَع إيصال دفعه بعد.» instead of «امتلأت المجموعة» about an order nobody paid — and it names a gateway capture first, because such an order also reads `pending`; and the **panel** shows «اعتمد» on `isApprovable()` only, in `OrderResource` and therefore on the queue that reuses its action. ⚠️ **`awaitingDecision()`/`isPending()` were NOT narrowed and must not be**: the credit ceiling, `RejectOrder`, `RedeemCoupon`'s one-undecided-order rule and `CancelSubscription` ask «has anybody decided this yet?», whose answer is still both statuses — narrowing it re-opens the ceiling defect. «ارفض» stays on `isPending()` so an abandoned `pending` order can still be closed. ⚠️ **The zero amount is the ONLY exception, and it is deliberate**: a 100% coupon, a fixed coupon clamped to zero, or a zero-price course sold through `CreateOrder` has no transfer to upload, and without it that order sits `pending` for ever. **The staff grant is NOT an exception**: `GrantCreditSubscription::grant()` calls `UploadPaymentReceipt` inside its own transaction, so the order it writes arrives `under_review` like any other — «fixing» that path with a `granted_by` bypass would open exactly the hole this closed. The API door already maps the `DomainException` to 422. Tests that approve only to exercise what approval DOES go through the `receiptUploaded()` Pest helper, which writes the status the upload writes; a test about the receipt itself goes through `UploadPaymentReceipt`.
- **A confined assistant's balances panel, plans and store goods are their OWN courses' (fixed 2026-09-30).** None of `billing.balance.view`, `plans.manage`, `store.items.manage` is on the default assistant role; an owner who ticked one onto a confined assistant opened the whole workspace to them. (1) `StudentBalanceController` hands `scopedCourseIdsFor()` to BOTH `ListStudentBalances` and `CountWithheldStudents` (a `?array $courseIds`, `null` = every course) — ⚠️ one narrowing for both reads, or the `withheld_students` card counts people the table never lists. (2) Plans: `PlanCourses::of()` is «which course does this plan sell» (course → itself, cohort → the group's course via `CohortDirectory::describeGroupCohort()`, workspace → none, so a workspace plan is refused to them); `SavePlan` asks `mayActOnCourse()` of the coverage the plan will HAVE (403 via `AuthorizationException`), `PlanPolicy::view/update` of the coverage it HAD, and the list and the change-request list go through `PlanCourses::within()`. (3) Store items: `course_id` is nullable — an item tied to no course is the teacher's shop at large and refused to them; `SaveStoreItem::handle(..., ?User $actor)` asks the target course, `StoreItemPolicy::view/update` the current one, and the list is `whereIn('course_id', $scoped)`. ⚠️ **Left alone, deliberately**: shipments (`store.shipments.manage` — an order's buyer need not be enrolled anywhere, so there is no course or scoped student to ask), rewards (`rewards.manage` — the teacher's points shop, workspace-level), redemptions (student-bound but not in this audit). `StudentBalanceAssistantScopeTest` · `PlanAndStoreAssistantScopeTest`.
