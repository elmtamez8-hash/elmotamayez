# Gotchas — Exams, questions, practice and grading

Moved verbatim from `CLAUDE.md` (the rule headlines stay there). Read this file before changing `Modules/Assessments/`, exams, the question bank, practice, study rooms. Bullets that say «above» may now point into another file in `docs/gotchas/`.

- **`concept_stats.lesson_id` is `NOT NULL` and zero means "the concept overall".** NULL never equals NULL, so a unique index carrying a nullable column does not bite on the one row every screen reads: `upsert()` matches nothing and INSERTs, a new row every night, and after a month the page shows whichever came back first — a number thirty days old while the correct one sits beside it, with not one error logged. `RollupIdempotencyTest` runs the job TWICE for that reason; one run is green for ever and proves the opposite. The rollup also excludes two families of row that are invisible in a naive `COUNT`: `is_practice` attempts (the flag is on `exam_attempts` while the answers are on `exam_answers`, so the join exists only for it), and essays with `requires_grading` and no `graded_at` — `GradeAttempt` writes those `is_correct = false` because no machine can judge them, so counting them reports every essay in the bank as 100% wrong, permanently. And a sample below `assessments.min_sample_size` stores `wrong_pct = NULL`, never zero: "nobody got this wrong" is what makes a teacher delete a good question two students happened to sit.
- **The snapshot is the denominator, and `attempt_items` is written at START.** Grading reads the snapshot, never the live question, so an exam edited after a student sat it cannot change what they were marked on. Two consequences that are not obvious: a migration had to BUILD those rows for every pre-existing attempt or each one's denominator is zero — and `SC-015` passes blind, because it measures `score` alone — and `GradeAttempt` writes an answer row for **every** item rather than for the answered ones, since a question left blank is the strongest evidence of a gap and would otherwise be missing from the mistake notebook entirely.
- **A question carries EXACTLY ONE correct option, and «at least one» was the rule until a teacher asked why two was allowed.** `GradeAttempt::matchesSnapshot()` compares the correct SET against the selected SET, and every answering surface in this product is single-select — so a second correct option is a question one tap can never satisfy: every student wrong for ever, and a `wrong_pct` of 100% that reads as the hardest item in the bank, which is the number a teacher deletes a good question over. **It is the same defect as NO correct option, reached from the opposite side**, which is why one rule refuses both. Guarded in `SaveQuestionRequest` and again in `SaveQuestion::guardExactlyOneCorrect()` — the importer, the seeder and the panel reach `syncOptions` with no form behind them — and named separately in `ImportQuestions`, because a per-row reason is read beside a row number and must say which COLUMN to edit. The three types are `mcq`, `true_false` and `essay`; none of them means "choose all that apply", and one that did would need its own type, its own student screen and its own partial-credit rule. The two answering surfaces (`PracticeRunner.tsx`, `exams/[uuid]/take/page.tsx`) replace the selection rather than accumulating it — they used to add, so a second tap silently turned a right answer into a zero on a graded paper.
- **The practice pool subtracts the exam not yet sat, and the notebook deliberately does NOT filter practice.** One bank question serves several exams, so a question missed last month can also be in next week's paper: practise it and the platform marks it instantly and shows the explanation, which is the exam handed over a week early. The mistake notebook is the mirror image — answering correctly IN a practice run is exactly how a mistake gets fixed, so filtering practice out means the "test me on my mistakes" loop can never resolve anything and returns the same questions for ever.
- **`claimForGrading()` outside the transaction strands the attempt for ever, and the refusal must stand BEFORE the claim.** `Attempt::claimForGrading()` is one conditional UPDATE moving `in_progress → grading`, and **nothing in the tree moves it back**. So a throw after it — a duplicate answer set colliding on `unique(attempt_id, question_id)`, a 500 anywhere in the loop — leaves the paper permanently unmarkable, with no sweep that notices and no screen that explains. `GradeAttempt` therefore refuses an attempt that already carries answer rows *above* the claim: refusing after it strands the attempt in exactly the state the refusal exists to prevent. The claim itself must still come before any write — reading a status and then acting on it is the race two taps win together, and a duplicated answer set double-counts every mistake in the notebook and doubles the denominator of every `wrong_pct`.
- **`PracticePool::withheldQuestionIds()` is computed PER STUDENT, so a room's door is «my own pool contains every question in it», never «I am enrolled here».** A host who has legitimately sat a published exam freezes its questions into their study room; every joiner who has *not* sat it then answers them and is handed the correct option ids and the explanation on the spot — next week's paper with its answers, which is precisely what the pool exists to prevent. The weaker predicate opens a second hole in the same line: `questionsFor()` is bounded by the student's own COURSES, not by the workspace, so a student enrolled in «العربي» would join a room built from the «الفيزياء» bank at the same teacher. `StudyRoomAccess::mayJoin()` counts the frozen questions this student may reach and compares against `study_rooms.question_count` — which is why that column stores what was actually DELIVERED and not what was asked for, and why the check is one query instead of a fetched id list counted in PHP.
