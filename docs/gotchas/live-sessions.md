# Gotchas — Live sessions, the broadcast room and attendance

Moved verbatim from `CLAUDE.md` (the rule headlines stay there). Read this file before changing `Modules/LiveSessions/`, LiveKit, join tickets, the room UI, attendance. Bullets that say «above» may now point into another file in `docs/gotchas/`.

- **Attendance never passes through the broadcast provider.** The register is built from a heartbeat hitting our own route and the server does the arithmetic: `stay_seconds += min(now − last_ping_at, 2 × interval)`. Three properties fall out of that one line — two devices do not double the time, leaving and returning aggregates into one stay, and an hour of silence is capped rather than credited. It is also why all of attendance was provable before any broadcast contract existed. A provider that reports its own attendance is a provider you cannot leave.
- **Seats are claimed by an atomic conditional UPDATE**, never `count()` then `insert()` — that is the definition of the race — and never `lockForUpdate()`, which is a no-op on SQLite, so a test written around it passes locally and proves nothing about the MySQL it will run on. `SeatConcurrencyTest` walks the same path real concurrency does.
- **`SessionCompleted` is not `SessionDelivered`.** Completion is "the session ended"; delivery is "the teacher joined, stayed long enough, and it ended normally". Two events rather than one with a flag, because a flag makes the condition optional for the listener — and only delivery carries the frozen seat count spec 006 bills against. `billable_seats` is likewise written **once**, at the cancellation deadline, and never recomputed: it answers a question about a moment that has passed, and a consumer that derives it from live bookings will get a different answer every day. ⚠️ **And ONE synchronous listener among queued ones is a single point of failure for everything dispatched after it.** `SessionDelivered` fires exactly once in a session's life — `CloseClassSession` returns early on a terminal status, and by then the transaction writing `status = completed` has committed — so a throw in a plain-class listener propagated back into the Action and killed the three lines below it: `SessionCompleted` (the teacher's counters *and* the recording ingest), `AttendanceConfirmed`, and the guardian report. All lost permanently, unrepeatable, with no sweep that notices. `AccrueUnitsOnDelivery` was that class while its Payments sibling was queued; both implement `ShouldQueueAfterCommit` now. ⚠️ **Not `ShouldQueue` + `ShouldHandleEventsAfterCommit`**, which is what they carried until 2026-09-25 and which does NOT wait: `Dispatcher::createClassCallable()` sends any `ShouldQueue` listener down the queued path before it ever reads `ShouldHandleEventsAfterCommit`, and with `after_commit => false` on every connection the job reached Redis inside the dispatching transaction — a worker could read pre-commit rows, or run for a transaction that rolled back. `ShouldHandleEventsAfterCommit` is for SYNC listeners only; `QueuedListenersAfterCommitTest` fails the build over the pairing. The throw it actually had was an unguarded `create()` in `compensateEmptySession()` — its sibling `accrueOne()` catches the duplicate because the unique index IS the idempotency guard, and the zero-seat row lands on that same index with the teacher standing in for the student.
- **The model is `ClassSession`, never `Session`.** `AuthSession` (spec 004) already owns that word, and two things called Session in one product is a line every reader misreads once. The frontend follows: `lib/class-sessions.ts` beside `lib/sessions.ts`.
- **A freeze period is read, never written to.** Scheduling, booking, `MarkAbsenteesJob` and `SyncTeacherCountersJob` consult it; no counter ever moves. That is why FR-043's "counters resume with their previous values" holds without a line of resumption code — there is nothing to undo. Sessions already booked inside one are suspended, not deleted. ⚠️ **A freeze on ONE student suspends only that student's INDIVIDUAL sessions; from a group session it takes their seat alone** (`CancelBooking::release()`, one notification). Until 2026-09-25 it suspended every group session the student sat in — every classmate's seat released and every classmate told «لن تُعقد» about a lesson still being taught — and `FreezePeriodTest` asserted exactly that, which is why it sat green. ⚠️ **And lifting a freeze is `DeleteFreezePeriod`, never `$period->delete()` from a screen**: `Suspended` had no way out, so a lifted freeze left its sessions dead for ever. The Action returns the still-future ones to `Scheduled` WITHOUT their seats (the holders were told it was off; a credit-paying student rebooks from «احجز», which revives their `Released` row — until 2026-09-25 it could not, because the unique `(class_session_id, student_user_id)` index refused the second INSERT and answered «لديك مقعد محجوز» about a seat they did not hold), and a generated 1:1 slot comes back with its booker's group taken off (`ClassSession::reopenEmptyIndividualSlot()`). ⚠️ **A GROUP seat a freeze on ONE student took — the lesson itself never stopped — comes back on the lift for a member who pays by credit or bought the course** (`DeleteFreezePeriod::restoreGroupSeats()`, the automation's own `reviveReleasedSeat()`; a room that filled up meanwhile is not forced, and the student and the teacher get `subscription_seat_unavailable` after the commit). Until 2026-09-25 only a subscriber got theirs back. ⚠️ **but a GROUP SUBSCRIBER gets theirs back without pressing anything (owner decision 2026-09-25): the delete moves the subscription's end earlier, and `EffectiveSubscriptionEnd` re-claims their seats up to the new end (reviving the rows the freeze `Released`, student freeze or workspace freeze alike) and announces `CourseAccessShortened`, on which `ReleaseSeatsOnSubscriptionEnd` releases the seats past the new end at once instead of at the nightly sweep. The delete and every revival are ONE transaction, and that is load-bearing: `RecomputeSubscriptionEnds` is `ShouldQueueAfterCommit`, and pushed at the bare delete it re-claimed while every suspended session was still `Suspended` — so a lifted workspace freeze reseated nobody (`FreezeLiftReseatTest`).** keeps any another period still covers or whose hour `SessionClash` now finds taken, and re-arms `FreezeBillableSeatsJob` — which does not skip a suspended session, so one whose deadline passed during the freeze already carries a frozen `billable_seats = 0`.
- **A booking row is the RECORD of a seat, not the seat — so a cancelled row must be revivable, and only by the right door (2026-09-25).** The row stays when the seat goes (its status is the history), and the unique `(class_session_id, student_user_id)` index then refused every re-booking with «لديك مقعد محجوز في هذه الحصة بالفعل». `BookSeat::claim()` now revives `cancelled_in_window` and `released` with ONE conditional UPDATE after `claimCapacity()` (`reviveRow()`, the same statement `reviveReleasedSeat()` uses), then places a fresh credit hold. ⚠️ **`cancelled_late` is UNDONE, never re-booked** (owner decision 2026-09-26): its seat is still in `seats_taken`, its hold still frozen, and it is still charged at delivery, so `BookSeat::handle()` puts the row back to `booked` through the same `reviveRow()` with NO capacity claim and NO new hold (`undoLateCancellation()`, before the lesson starts only; the screen's button reads «تراجع عن الإلغاء» and ignores `seats.available`, because the chair it returns to is already the student's). Claiming either again would sell one chair twice. ⚠️ **And every revival clears `reminded_at`, `excused_at` and `excused_by_user_id`**: the reminder job claims `reminded_at IS NULL`, so a row reminded before it was cancelled was never reminded of the lesson it was booked back into, and an excuse belonged to the absence the cancellation was, not to the seat now held. A lifted WORKSPACE freeze tells each non-subscriber whose group seat it released that the lesson is back (`session_seat_reopened`, one notice per student) and books nothing for them. ⚠️ **And the automation still skips a student's own cancellation** (`ClaimSubscriptionSeats::decide()`, FR-044): the widened set is the button's, not the night's. ⚠️ **A generated 1:1 slot is filed under its booker's one-seat group at booking, and `cohort_from_booking` says the SEAT wrote it** — when the seat goes (in-time cancel, system release, freeze lift) `reopenEmptyIndividualSlot()` takes the group off in one conditional UPDATE guarded on `seats_taken = 0`; a 1:1 session a teacher scheduled FOR one student (a granted private request) carries `false` and keeps its group. Rows stamped before the column existed also carry `false` and stay stamped. The student's own «احجز» on a 1:1 slot also asks `LeadTime` (the private-request rule); a teacher's grant and the automation do not. ⚠️ **And a freeze's days are the PLATFORM's days**: `FreezePeriod::scopeCovering()` and `ClassSession::scopeStartingInside()` read `sessions.timezone` — at UTC midnight a 01:00 Doha lesson on the first frozen day stayed bookable and one on the day after was suspended (`FreezeDayBoundaryTest`).
- **`SESSIONS_VIEW` and `ATTENDANCE_VIEW` are two different questions.** The first says "you may see the sessions you can book" and every student holds it; the second says "you may read who attended one". Authorising the register with the first hands each student the class roll — names, stay durations, and the reason a teacher changed someone's mark. A reader without `ATTENDANCE_VIEW` gets their own row, which is ownership rather than permission and therefore not a 403.
- **`SessionCancelled` carries the seat holders, because by the time a listener runs nobody holds a seat.** Releasing them is part of cancelling, so a listener that reads the bookings afterwards sees one undifferentiated pile and tells the student who cancelled last month that their session is off. The Action reads the ids while they are still true.
- **`teacher_profiles.attendance_rate` is the TEACHER's attendance**, not their students'. It is the share of countable sessions actually delivered; a student's absence never touches it. The name reads the other way, which is why the meaning is written in `SyncTeacherCountersJob`, in `docs/README.md`, and in a test that fails if anyone changes it.
- **`DomainException` extends `LogicException`, so a controller catching `RuntimeException` lets it through to a 500 — and `CancelClassSession` never stamps `room_closed_at`.** So `joinWindowCovers()` still said yes on a cancelled session, `OpenBroadcastRoom` was reached, and a teacher who cancelled a lesson and tapped «دخول الغرفة» got a raw error page — with the heartbeat asking for another every thirty seconds. Both `join()` and `presence()` catch both hierarchies now, and `IssueJoinTicket`'s `@throws` names both: PHPStan reads that annotation as the complete list, so a lying one makes the correct catch look dead. ⚠️ **And three of the five provider methods rethrew `TwirpError` raw**, including `applyToWholeRoom()`'s participant list which stood outside any `try` at all — one LiveKit blip during «اكتم الجميع» was a 500 carrying the vendor's transport vocabulary, the exact defect `createRoom()` had already been fixed for. `translate()` is the one spelling: `NotFound` becomes a `DomainException` and a sentence *when the caller has one to give*, everything else becomes `BroadcastProviderUnavailable` ⇒ 503, caught ABOVE the other arms.
- **Opening the broadcast room was a read-then-write, and a double tap bought a parent two reports for one hour.** Two host tabs put two workers in `OpenBroadcastRoom` at once; both read `broadcast_room_id` as null, both wrote, and both dispatched a `CloseClassSessionJob` — which both fire at the same instant, both read `status = live`, and both close, while `SendSessionReport` stamps `report_sent_at` AFTER it dispatches. The claim is a conditional UPDATE and only the winner schedules the timeline; never `lockForUpdate()`, a no-op on SQLite. ⚠️ **A sequential «open it twice» test passes against a build with no claim in it** — the second call returns at the `!== null` line one step earlier — which is how this was nearly shipped green. `createRoom()` is asked BETWEEN the read and the write, so a `onCreateRoom` callback that claims the row IS the other worker winning inside that window: no threads, no sleeps. Same seam `onRecording` opens for the ingest claim.
- **«إخراج» takes the video and the room's CHAT, and the seat is deliberately untouched by both.** Cancelling the booking would repossess a session the student PAID for over a moment's behaviour, so no seat predicate can see a removal — and `ConversationPolicy` asked only about the seat, so the removed student carried straight on typing the same thing into the room chat that every classmate reads. The teacher's only remaining instrument was a workspace-wide BAN: recorded, appealable, covering every thread with them for ever, and the wrong weight for one hour. Locking the room silences the whole class to reach one person. `wasRemovedFromSession()` sits on `SessionAttendanceDirectory` beside `hasSeatInSession()`, is read for that room ONLY, exempts `chat.moderate` exactly as the lock does (a teacher has an attendance row of their own, so a bulk clear could otherwise mute a second teacher), and never touches reading or the private thread.
- **A provider error is an ANSWER, and both shapes of it were a 500.** Translation happens inside the adapter — the one file allowed to know the vendor's name (017 FR-002) — never in a controller, which a `TwirpError` caught upstairs would fill with the vendor's vocabulary. Two cases, two answers: a participant who left a second before the teacher pressed mute is `NotFound` ⇒ `DomainException` ⇒ 422 with a sentence (the most ordinary event in a live lesson, and it read «حدث خطأ»); an unreachable provider is `BroadcastProviderUnavailable` ⇒ **503**, caught **above** the uniform `RuntimeException` arm it inherits from, or the module's «you may not enter» swallows it and sends a teacher to check a booking that was never the problem. Naming an outage leaks nothing — FR-015's uniform refusal is about ENTITLEMENT, which varies with who is asking; an outage does not. The money half of that step was already right and must stay: no room, no `room_opened_at`, and zero rows in `credit_transactions`/`teaching_units`/`ledger_entries`.
- **The unlock gate guards booking and joining, and deliberately not the nightly sweep.** `BookingEligibility::openingRefusal()` is the new door; `refusalReason()` stays separate because it is the question a seat-**releasing** caller asks — folding the homework condition into it would repossess a paid seat over an unfinished worksheet. (`ReleaseIneligibleBookings`, the nightly sweep that asked it, had no production caller and was deleted 2026-09-23 — specs 006 and 027 had already decided against it.) Excused counts as attended (only `absent` fails, an excusal being the teacher's decision that the absence is not held against the student), "previous" skips cancelled and suspended sessions, and a submitted-but-unmarked homework satisfies a score threshold: the late party there is the teacher.
- **The heartbeat's query budget guarded the cheap third of it.** `QueryBudgetTest` measured `RecordPresencePing` directly (≤6) while `BroadcastController::presence()` re-runs the WHOLE of `IssueJoinTicket`, then the ping, then a `refresh()` — about twenty-one, with the balances read TWICE per beat (once by the withholding check, once by the prepaid fall-through beneath it). Thirty students twice a minute is over a thousand queries a minute per room. Three fixes and each is a rule: `isWithheld()` reads the balances once and stamps ONE of them (the bulk form is for the panel and is a different question); `IssueJoinTicket` asks the CLOCK before the eligibility chain, which is safe precisely because every refusal there is the same refusal (FR-015), so order leaks nothing; and the endpoint is now measured beside the Action with a **deliberately tight** ceiling — the budgets above it carry twice their fixture because they measure LISTS whose cost must not grow with rows, while this endpoint has no rows, so any increase is a new read rather than a bigger one. ⚠️ `PresenceLoop` also never stopped: it counted failures and kept posting for the life of the tab, so a page left open after the lesson bought the full chain twice a minute for ever. And `ParticipantsPanel` refetched on every change of the participant COUNT — every client watching every arrival, so a thirty-seat room filling up was 465 unthrottled requests in two minutes.
- **An enum value with three READERS and no writer is a requirement everybody believed was implemented.** `ClassSessionStatus::Interrupted` was selected by `CloseStaleSessionsJob`, counted in the denominator by `SyncTeacherCountersJob` and excluded from the numerator by `countsTowardsCounters()` — and **nothing in the tree could ever write it**. What it hid: every job that ends a session (`MarkAbsenteesJob`, `CloseClassSessionJob`) is dispatched from `OpenBroadcastRoom`, so a teacher who never opened the room had NOTHING scheduled against their session, and the sweep did not select `scheduled` either. Seats held for ever, no register, no guardian report, and — the sharp half — **the counter moved the wrong way**: a teacher who opened and left early was marked down, while one who never turned up was in neither side of the ratio. Missing the lesson entirely was cheaper than teaching half of it, which is `FR-062` inverted. `AbandonClassSession` is the missing writer and is deliberately **not a branch inside `CloseClassSession`**: that Action's first act is a provider call to close a room, and its register marks every seat holder Absent — which `SendSessionReportsJob` would then send to every guardian, about a class nobody held. It also does not dispatch `SessionCompleted` (whose other listener would interrogate a provider about a room that never existed, and an empty egress list is indistinguishable from «still encoding» ⇒ two days of sweeps then `failed`); the counter sync is asked for directly, because it is the one real consequence. ⚠️ **And the discriminator is the STATUS, not `room_opened_at`** — the two agree in a real database, where only `OpenBroadcastRoom` writes `Live` and it writes both in one statement, but fixtures stamp `live` without a timestamp, so branching on the timestamp turns forty existing tests into confident claims about a lesson that never happened. **The sweep's per-row `try/catch` ships with it**: adding a status widens the list it walks, and one unresolvable `broadcast_provider` used to kill the whole hourly sweep on its first row — the net that exists to catch a lost close, lost to one bad row. A test of that must create the poison row FIRST, or it passes against exactly that bug.
- **The first REAL session against a live LiveKit found five defects in one run (2026-08-18), and every one of them was invisible to a suite that fakes the vendor.**
  - **One configured URL serves two protocols.** `LIVEKIT_URL` is `wss://` because the BROWSER dials it and the ticket carries it on untouched — while `RoomServiceClient` and `EgressServiceClient` speak Twirp over HTTP and refuse anything else at the transport (`TwirpError: failed to send request: The scheme 'wss' is not supported`). So no room had ever opened, and the student read «تعذّر الدخول» over a message naming no URL. `apiUrl()` derives the HTTP form rather than adding a second env var: two URLs for one host disagree eventually, and then tickets point somewhere nothing was provisioned. Every adapter test injects both clients, which is exactly why none reached the line that builds one.
  - **⚠️ THE SERVER FILLS THE DEPRECATED `file`, NOT `file_results` — and reading only the plural one loses recordings SILENTLY.** We request `file_outputs`, so `file_results` is what should come back; LiveKit Cloud returned it absent with the whole result under `file` (15.5 MB, `EGRESS_COMPLETE`). An empty `file_results` on a complete egress is indistinguishable from "still encoding", so the sweep would retry to the attempt limit, mark the session failed, and **release the teacher's held fee against a file sitting intact in our own bucket** — with nothing logged. Both fields are read, plural first. The library announces the deprecation while PARSING the response, before any accessor is touched, so reaching for it adds noise we already carry and no new risk.
  - **The egress LAYOUT is a decision; unset means somebody else makes it.** The default template starts on `grid` and switches itself to `speaker` the moment a screen is shared, which reserves a participant sidebar — empty when the camera is off, so a shared screen landed in the left two-thirds of a 1280×720 frame, letterboxed, the rest black. `single-speaker` renders the dominant track alone, edge to edge. It is also what keeps other participants' cameras out of a file published to everyone who booked: a grid would put a student's room into a permanent lesson recording, a privacy decision nobody made on purpose.
  - **The host has an attendance row ON PURPOSE, and it is not a student row.** `CloseClassSession` judges delivery — the teacher's pay — from it, so removing it stops every session from being billable. What must not happen is rendering it in the class roll: the register showed «Demo Teacher» marked absent beside a manual-attendance control and a note field labelled «تصل وليّ الأمر». `SendSessionReportsJob` already skipped it in a loop; one rule in two places is how the second place gets it wrong, so it is `Attendance::scopeExcludingHost()` now and both read it.
  - **A recording is entitled by the SEAT in both directions, and only one was implemented.** `accessTo()` had long refused to let a recording STAND in front of another item (the forever-bug that locks a course behind an hour the student was never in) — but the recording ITSELF was still gated on the course sequence, so a student who booked and PAID for a session was told «أكمِل … أولاً» about the lesson that session produced, while `IssuePlaybackGrant::mayWatch()` said yes. Two doors disagreeing, and the recording opens only from the closed one. It refuses with `NO_SEAT`, never `SEQUENCE`: the first means "later", the second means "not yours", and nothing can be completed to open it.
- **A session stays `live` after its room closes, and the badge must not repeat that.** `CloseClassSessionJob` runs at `ends_at` + the join window so a teacher who shuts their laptop does not leave a session live for ever — which puts a teacher who ends the broadcast DELIBERATELY in the same gap: room deleted and unopenable, status still `live`. The page badged a finished lesson «جارية» and kept offering «دخول الغرفة», which answers «تعذّر الدخول». `room_closed` is sent as a boolean (the closing time is nobody's business on a card) and outranks the status on both the badge and the button.
- **`AccessToken`'s default ttl is FOUR HOURS — and the SDK's own docblock says six, which is where the wrong number in this file came from.** `vendor/agence104/livekit-server-sdk/src/AccessToken.php` reads `protected int $ttl = 4 * 60 * 60;` two lines under a comment claiming "Defaults to 6 hours". Read the assignment, never the comment above it; a vendor comment is not a vendor guarantee. The assertion guarding our own ttl was separately measuring backwards: the contract test asserted `$ticket->expiresAt->diffInHours(now())` is `< 24` — on a *future* date Carbon returns a negative number, so it passed for any ttl whatsoever, including none. A forgotten `setTtl()` would have shipped green with a ticket that opens the room hours after the lesson ended. It now measures `now()->diffInMinutes($expiresAt)` against `SessionSettings::ticketTtlMinutes()`, not a literal — the ttl is a `platform_settings` row an operator tunes, and a hard-coded ceiling fails the gate over a legitimate config change.
- **The API KEY travels in every ticket and the SECRET never does — a criterion banning both is a criterion that cannot pass.** `LIVEKIT_API_KEY` is the JWT's `iss` claim, which is how the server knows which key to verify against; it opens nothing alone. `LIVEKIT_API_SECRET` signs. `SC-005` was written as "zero provider key in any payload", which is false by protocol — and a test written to it would have been deleted rather than maintained. Same family as `room_url`, which the browser *must* have or nothing connects.
- **LiveKit cannot revoke a ticket and RECREATES a deleted room on the first join, so FR-009 is kept by two guards of OURS — and simplifying either one silently reopens a closed lesson.** There is no revocation call in the protocol, so a ticket still inside its ttl rebuilds the room `CloseBroadcastRoom` just deleted. ⚠️ **Auto-create IS controllable on Cloud, contrary to what this line said until it was checked against a real dashboard** — «Automatically create rooms on participant join» is a project-level checkbox under Settings → General, and it ships **ON**. Turning it off removes the rebuild entirely, but it does not remove the need for the two guards: they are what makes the behaviour correct on a self-hosted server, on a project where somebody re-enables it, and in the window before `OpenBroadcastRoom` has run. What actually holds the door shut is (1) `IssueJoinTicket` refusing to mint anything once `room_closed_at` is set, and (2) `BroadcastController` re-running that same issuance on **every `presence` heartbeat**, which evicts whoever is inside. The residual exposure is one heartbeat interval in an otherwise empty room, and it is deliberate. `TicketAfterCloseTest` is the only place this is written down — a reader who takes the heartbeat re-check for a redundant query and removes it will not see the room reopen, because it only reopens for someone holding a live ticket.
- **An eligibility endpoint must ask whether the caller is the host before answering.** `BookingEligibility` answers questions about a STUDENT — an enrolment, a balance, a piece of homework — and no teacher holds any of them in their own workspace. So `/eligibility` told the host of a session «لست مسجّلاً عند هذا المدرّس», and the room page printed it under a refusal banner on their own lesson while the actual refusal was usually the clock. The host check is `Gate::allows('host', $session)` — the same `ClassSessionPolicy` ability the host routes are gated on in `BroadcastController` (not inside `PerformHostAction`, which carries no authorization of its own), never a re-derived condition, because two spellings of "is this the host" put one answer on the screen and another at the door.
- **A bare `audio` / `video` on `LiveKitRoom` means PUBLISH ON CONNECT, and it put a child's bedroom in a lesson recording.** Both props default to `false` in the library and were passed bare, i.e. `true` — so a student opening the room had her camera on the class stage and in the egress file before touching anything, under a `RecordingNotice` saying «إن لم ترغب في الظهور، أغلِقِ الكاميرا»: the opt-out offered a moment after the choice was taken, which that component's own docblock calls not-a-notice. It is `ticket.role === "host"` for both now — a teacher pressing two buttons before the class can hear them is the first thirty seconds of every lesson spent on plumbing — and the role comes from the SIGNED ticket, so it cannot be flipped from the browser. Only a component test sees this: the backend does not know the prop, and Playwright would have to inspect a real connection. ⚠️ **The neighbouring `onError` was `err.message`** — the library's own English, in an Alert title, under an Arabic heading, on the failure most likely to happen on mobile data — with `userMessage()` imported nine lines below it.
- **`void promise` is not error handling, and the two halves of that fail differently.** `BroadcastStage`'s microphone, camera and screen buttons were `onClick={() => void localParticipant.setCameraEnabled(...)}`. A rejected promise there is an unhandled rejection: in development the overlay paints a raw `TypeError` over the lesson, and **in production nothing happens at all** — no message, no reason, on the one control a student needs to be seen. Measured on a real phone (2026-08-26): `Cannot read properties of undefined (reading 'getUserMedia')`, because outside a secure context the browser does not expose `navigator.mediaDevices` **at all**. That case is named SEPARATELY from every other failure and must stay that way: nothing was refused and no permission dialog will ever appear, so «تعذّر الوصول للكاميرا» sends the student hunting a setting that is not the problem — the fault is the address the page was opened from. `localhost` is a secure origin and a LAN IP is not, so this is invisible on the machine that serves the page and certain on every other device. **Only a component test can see any of it**: the backend does not know the button, and Playwright cannot take `navigator.mediaDevices` away from a browser.
- **The join ticket carries a uuid and never a name (`FR-006`), so the participant list is a JOIN, not a payload.** The identity is echoed by the provider to everyone in the room, which is why the name stays out of it — and the consequence went unnoticed for two specs: `participant.name || participant.identity` rendered a raw uuid for every row of every lesson, because the name is never set. `GET /class-sessions/{session}/participants` answers the names, faces and badges over our own authenticated route. Two rules come with it. It is **not the register**: `ATTENDANCE_VIEW` guards who attended, for how long and why a mark changed, while every seat holder holds this — so a status, a stay or a note here hands each student their classmates' record. And the frontend draws **only who is connected**, because the roster deliberately answers for everyone who *could* be present (bookings ∪ attendance ∪ the teacher, so an assistant with no seat is named too) and rendering it whole would publish the roll of everybody who did not show up. The raised hand beside a name is a LiveKit **attribute** — replayed to whoever joins later, unlike a data message — enabled by `setCanUpdateOwnMetadata(true)` on the grant, and it is read as a boolean and drawn as an icon: attributes are written by the client that owns them, so anything printed from one is that person's keyboard on everybody else's screen.
- **The RECORDER is a participant, so «إخراج الجميع» would have evicted it — and no test could have said so.** The egress joins the LiveKit room under its own identity (`EG_…`, `kind = EGRESS`), verified in a live participant list on 2026-08-26, so a bulk host action that loops over everyone stops the recording mid-lesson and tries to mute a robot. `LiveKitBroadcastProvider::applyToWholeRoom()` skips anything that is not `Kind::STANDARD`, and skips the ACTOR too: a teacher who removes themselves leaves the room open, the recording running and nobody inside who can close it. That is why `hostAction()` takes an `$actor` as well as a `$target` — the provider is the only place that sees the list a bulk action walks. Two more rules fall out: a bulk action is ONE request and never a loop at the caller (twenty presses is twenty chances for one to fail in the middle, leaving the room half muted with nothing saying which half), and `NotFound` inside the loop is skipped per participant rather than raised — the list is a snapshot, somebody closing their laptop between two iterations means the action already happened. Measured end to end against a live project: after `remove-all` the student was gone, the teacher stayed, and the egress was still recording.
- **«إخراج» was a provider call and nothing else, so it lasted one refresh.** Removing a participant disconnected them; the room stayed open, the seat stayed booked, and `IssueJoinTicket` minted them a fresh ticket a second later — the teacher's one control over a disruptive student cost them an F5. Reported from a real lesson, 2026-08-26. The refusal is recorded on `attendances.removed_at` and read at the door. **It is not the booking**: cancelling the seat would repossess a session the student PAID for over a moment's behaviour, and the money half of that is a refund nobody asked for — this says «you are out of THIS hour», which is what the teacher meant. **The host is never read against it**: a teacher has an attendance row of their own (`CloseClassSession` judges delivery from it), so a check above the role branch lets one host lock another out of their own lesson with the button meant for a student. **And the stamp comes from what the provider CONFIRMS** — `hostAction()` returns the identities it acted on — because only the provider sees who was in the room, and re-deriving "who is present" from `last_ping_at` beside `RecordPresencePing`'s own arithmetic is the two-spellings defect whose failure direction is this very bug surviving for whoever the heuristic missed. Readmission is ours and never reaches the provider (there is nothing for it to do), for the same reason `End` is: routed through `hostAction()` it would answer 501 on a button that needs no provider at all. Measured against a live project: remove → join `403`, readmit → join `200`, and the browser does not reconnect on its own.
- **The room's three student signals are all CLIENT-WRITTEN, which decides how each one may be used.** `hand` and `confused` are LiveKit participant attributes — replayed to whoever joins later, unlike a data message, and needing no endpoint of ours because a raised hand is worth nothing once the lesson ends. Three rules follow. **Nothing is rendered from them as text**: the owner writes them, so anything printed is that person's keyboard on everybody else's screen — each key is compared to `"1"` and drawn as an icon. **Off is an empty string, never `"0"`**: the provider DELETES an empty attribute, and the teacher's «امسح الإشارات» writes the same empty string server-side, so «lowered» has one spelling instead of two. **And the QUEUE ORDER is observed, not sent** — a timestamp inside the attribute is the obvious design and the wrong one, because the student who wants to be first would simply say they were, and being asked in turn is the whole point of the feature; the panel numbers hands by the moment THIS screen was told, which a browser cannot forge. The price is written down: after the teacher reloads, a roomful of already-raised hands is seen at once and numbered arbitrarily. ⚠️ And a test of that ordering must make the FIRST raiser lower and raise again — written the obvious way (two people, two raises) it passes against a build with no ordering in it at all, because insertion order and raise order coincide.
- **«لم أفهم» is not a second raised hand, and that is the whole reason it exists.** Raising a hand is a request to speak in front of the class, which is exactly what the lost student will not do. It carries no text — a free-form «ما فهمت إيه؟» is a second unmoderated chat in the middle of a lesson — and what the teacher reads is a COUNT in the header, because mid-explanation the actionable fact is that four people are lost, not which four. Neither signal goes through `SelfControls::run()`: that guard refuses when `navigator.mediaDevices` is missing, and these two controls are the ones that still work for a student who cannot publish anything — which is precisely the student who most needs to ask.
- **«The previous session» means the previous one IN THIS STUDENT'S GROUP.** The unlock condition and the attendance check walk backwards from a session; without `class_sessions.cohort_id` in that predicate the Sunday student is told «حضورُك ناقصٌ في حصّة» about Saturday's lesson — one they never saw, were never booked into, and can do nothing about. The column is nullable because every session predating the group carries null, so the comparison is `sameCohort()` rather than an equality that treats two nulls as different rooms.
- **Hiding an unassigned session from discovery must never swallow a seat that was paid for.** Creating the first group of a course hides every `cohort_id IS NULL` session from the picker (Q3) — and a student may already HOLD one of them. ⚠️ **`CohortSessionVisibility` has TWO arms, not three, and this line used to claim a third.** Measured against the code on 2026-09-02: it is «my groups' sessions **∪** the unassigned ones of a course that has no groups», and nothing in that predicate reads a booking. The promise is real and is kept ELSEWHERE — `GetStudentSchedule` is built from the student's own BOOKINGS and is deliberately untouched by this predicate (FR-025د), so a seat already held stays on their timetable whatever discovery decides. The demo seeder ships a booked unassigned session for exactly this reason. **A rule that lives only in a document is a rule the next reader implements from the document** — and this one had been copied into `docs/erd.md` as well.
- **THE HEARTBEAT ASKS WHAT CAN EVICT, NOT WHAT LET YOU IN — AND CONFLATING THE TWO COST 15 QUERIES A BEAT AND THREW PAYING STUDENTS OUT OF LESSONS.** The broadcast provider cannot revoke a ticket and recreates a deleted room on the first join, so re-asking on every `presence` beat is our only instrument for removing somebody — **that re-ask is load-bearing and must not be deleted** (`TicketAfterCloseTest`). But `BroadcastController::presence()` called `IssueJoinTicket::handle()` *whole*, so twice a minute per participant it re-ran the enrolment, the freeze period, the withholding chain, the credit holds and the unlock rule: measured at **15 queries**, i.e. ~900 a minute in a thirty-seat room. ⚠️ **And it was not only cost**: a student whose balance flickered mid-explanation was ejected from an hour she had already paid for — behaviour no requirement asks for, and a side effect of reusing the door's function rather than a decision. `LiveSessions\Support\RoomRevocation` is the split, and the line is **«can this change during the lesson, and should it eject her?»**: the window, `room_closed_at`, a terminal status, the seat, and the host's removal — yes; enrolment, freeze, money and homework — no, those were settled at the door. Measured after: **5**, and the budget ceiling moved **down** to 6. ⚠️ **One class owns the questions and BOTH doors ask it** (`IssueJoinTicket` calls it too, then adds `maySit` underneath) — two copies would be the «two doors disagreeing» defect this file records for `BookingEligibility`, `ListLeaderboardScopes` and spec 018's paid recording. ⚠️ **The terminal-status check is not redundant beside the window check**, and the suite is what proved it: `CancelClassSession` never stamps `room_closed_at`, so a cancelled session passes `joinWindowCovers()` completely — before that line the beat answered **200** on a cancelled lesson, because the refusal had been arriving by accident from `OpenBroadcastRoom` inside the host branch. ⚠️ **And a stale comment is how the drift hid**: `frozenCreditRefusal()` and `SessionCreditHolds::availableFor()` both cited «a budget of 15 against a steady state of 14» — while that method's own query is what had turned the 14 into a 15. A docblock that cites a measurement must be re-measured when the code around it moves, or it describes the world before itself.
- **«شاهد التسجيل لاحقاً» had a reader and no writer — `RecordRecordingWatched` was called by one test and nothing else, so the register line never appeared for anybody (found 2026-09-25).** It is written now from the playback RENEWAL loop: `RenewPlaybackGrant` fires Media's `PlaybackSustained` once per grant, on the renewal whose SERVER-clock age (`now − grant.created_at`) first crosses `media.watched_share` of the asset's duration (fallback `media.watched_fallback_seconds` when the duration is unknown; the LiveKit egress reports one), and `RecordWatchedOnSustainedPlayback` claims it only for the primary file of a lesson with a `class_session_id`. ⚠️ **Never "simplify" the signal to `position_seconds` or `renewed_count`**: the first is whatever the client typed, and nothing enforces a gap between renewals, so a script runs the second to any number in a second. ⚠️ **The Action's write is `UPDATE … WHERE recording_watched_at IS NULL` and it skips the host** — the teacher has an attendance row on purpose and may watch their own recording, and without the skip their row would read «شاهد التسجيل لاحقاً» about the lesson they taught. It moves no `status` and no money (FR-021د · SC-023). `RecordingWatchedWiringTest` drives the real routes and was shown to fail with the `Event::listen` line removed, the host guard removed, and the `whereNull` removed.
- **EVERY TIME IS SHOWN ON THE READER'S OWN CLOCK, AND THE PLATFORM ZONE DECIDES ONLY WHERE A DAY BEGINS (owner decision 2026-09-25).** The product has users in Qatar (UTC+3 all year) and Egypt (UTC+3 until 2026-10-29, then UTC+2). Until then every screen drew session times on the platform zone (`session.timezone`, Asia/Qatar) while the private-session request form drew its buttons on the browser's — so from 2026-10-29 a Cairo student picked 17:00 and read 18:00 in «حصصي». Now: the frontend draws every session/booking/schedule time through `useViewerTimeZone()` (`lib/viewer-time-zone.ts`) — `users.timezone`, else the browser's — and the server prints every time in a notification through `UserClock::format()` / `SessionSettings::formatFor()`, which names the zone («… (توقيت مصر)»). ⚠️ **`users.timezone` was written only by the quiet-hours form**, so `AuthProvider` stamps the browser's zone once when it is empty (`PUT /me/timezone` with `only_if_unset` — a conditional UPDATE, so it never overwrites a zone somebody chose). ⚠️ **A fan-out listener computes the time INSIDE the per-recipient loop** — `NotifySeatHolders`, `NotifySessionRescheduleDecided` and `BookSubscribersOnScheduled` used to format once before it. ⚠️ **`session.timezone` on the resources is still the PLATFORM zone**, kept for compatibility; no screen should draw a time on it. ⚠️ **Not converted, on purpose or by scope**: `BookSeat`/`BookingEligibility` print `first_release_at` in UTC (booking logic owned elsewhere), and `EloquentCohortScheduleDirectory`'s «السبت ٥م» group labels are server-built strings on the platform zone.
- **A weekly availability window is WALL-CLOCK TIME + THE TEACHER'S IANA ZONE, never UTC (2026-09-25).** A weekly window stored in UTC cannot express a zone that observes DST: the old writer converted «Tuesday 17:00» with the browser's offset of the current week, so a Cairo teacher's row said «Tuesday 14:00 UTC» and `GenerateSessionsFromAvailability` put the lesson at 16:00 on their clock every winter. `availability_slots.timezone` now names the clock, the editors send what was typed plus `timezone` (**required** — a stale tab would otherwise send UTC hours that would be read as local), and every reader converts PER DATE in the slot's zone: `AvailabilitySlot::occurrenceOn()` (the generator, which reads `from`/`to` as the teacher's calendar dates, inclusive), `containsSpan()` (`RequestPrivateSession`), `covers()` and `scopeCovering()` («متاح الآن» — one ORed clause per DISTINCT zone, never `CONVERT_TZ()`), and `lib/availability.ts` (`startsWithin`, `toViewerSlot`). ⚠️ **A slot with no `timezone` in a PAYLOAD is an old cached UTC response**, and the client reads it as UTC; a ROW with a null zone reads as the platform zone. ⚠️ **The backfill (`2026_09_25_000200`) reproduces THIS WEEK's instants**: each UTC row is converted with the offset its zone has on this week's date, stamped `users.timezone` of the teacher else `Asia/Qatar`; a window that crosses local midnight is SPLIT (first half ends `23:59:59`), and `teacher_applications.step_data.step_4` — a second copy of the same rows — is converted and stamped too. ⚠️ **A Cairo teacher whose account had no stored zone was stamped `Asia/Qatar`**: right today (both UTC+3), an hour out from 2026-10-29 until they save their week once, which stamps their browser's zone.
- **A zone the person CHOSE outranks every browser report, and «two clocks» is decided by the OFFSET at the instant (owner decisions 2026-09-26).** `users.timezone_source` is `manual` (the «المنطقة الزمنية» card in `/settings`) or `browser` (the sign-in stamp, the quiet-hours form). The one writer is `App\Shared\Actions\RecordAccountTimezone` — in `Shared` because the quiet-hours controller in `Notifications` writes through it too — and a `browser` write is a conditional UPDATE that never touches a `manual` row; until the person chooses, the stored zone FOLLOWS the browser. ⚠️ **The quiet-hours form used to write `timezone` unconditionally — and null when absent** — so saving quiet hours undid a choice; it now reports the browser zone through the same writer or not at all. ⚠️ **Private-request and reschedule messages and screens print both clocks** («… 17:00 بتوقيتك · 18:00 بتوقيت المدرّس») through `UserClock::formatBoth()` / `formatSessionTimeBoth()` — ONLY when the two offsets differ at that instant: Cairo and Doha are two names for one hour until 2026-10-29, and a name comparison would print the same hour twice. The resources send `counterpart_timezone` (the teacher's to the student, the student's to the teacher); the lists eager-load it, the reschedule one unscoped through `teacherProfile`. ⚠️ **A group's meeting slots travel as INSTANTS as well as labels** (`CohortScheduleDirectory::scheduleSlotsFor()` → `schedule_slots`, the next meeting in each slot): a weekday and a clock time cannot be converted — «السبت ٥م» Doha is 16:00 or 17:00 in Cairo depending on the date — so `CohortScheduleSlots` draws the instant on the visitor's clock, and the platform-zone `schedule` text stays for the server render and crawlers. Teacher-side screens still get labels only.
